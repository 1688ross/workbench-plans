## 1. The concept in one paragraph

An **L-shaped island** with one continuous 2-1/4" hard-maple top at exactly the
SawStop's table height. The long leg (96" x 48") is the outfeed table, the
workbench and the router table; the short leg (36" x 96") is the miter station
and the glue-up area. Every built-in tool has its own zone, and the zones are
placed so that **no two machines are neighbours**: the dog-hole workbench is at
the west end, the router is in the south-east quarter, the miter saw is at the
far end of the wing, and the outfeed strip runs along the north edge between
them. Everything that can retract, retracts: the router lift drops below the
plate, the router fence lives in a drawer, the miter extension telescopes
into the wing, and the clamps slide into a "garage" in the base. What you see
when nothing is in use is one big flat slab of maple over charcoal cabinets.

What makes it unusual:

- **The dog-hole field is also the downdraft sanding table.** The 3/4" holes
  open into a sealed 6"-tall plenum under the west end of the top. That plenum
  gives holdfasts the clearance they need, and it has a 4" blast gate to the
  dust trunk. Open the gate and the whole 36" x 40" field pulls sanding dust
  down through the holes. No separate perforated panel to store.
- **A clamp garage instead of a clamp rack.** The north strip of the island
  base (the side that faces the table saw and is otherwise dead space) is a
  17"-deep, 96"-long tunnel with a door on the west end. Parallel clamps and
  pipe clamps slide in flat on rails, like drawers of clamps.
- **One utility corner.** Dust trunk outlet and the power inlet leave the bench
  at the same north-east corner, so there is one hose and one cord to the
  outside world.
- **The alcove.** The inside of the L is a 96" x 48" pocket of floor. You stand
  in it for the vises, the router and the miter saw, with the table saw behind
  the island. It concentrates your walking into one place.

## 2. Station layout

| **B** Outfeed | Island, north 18" strip, x = 44"–96" | Flat continuation of the saw table. Two 20"-long grooves aligned with the miter slots so a miter gauge or sled runs off the saw. Top sits 1/16" below the saw. | Always flat. |

Assembly squares and bench cookies in the wing drawers. The 87" run of wing top north of the miter blade is also the miter saw's left-hand board support. | Always flat. |
| **E** Miter station | Wing, south half | 12" saw in a well, bed flush with the top. Full-height hood behind the saw (east side) with a 4" drop plus a 2-1/2" hose to the saw's own port. 36" telescoping extension pulls out of the wing's south end. A stop-track rule along the wing's fence line. | The saw stays up (see question 2). Extension, hood chute and fence stop all stow. |
| Clamp garage | Island base, north strip, door on the west end | Parallel clamps, pipe clamps, long F-clamps on slide-out rails. | Yes. |
| Utility corner | Island NE corner | 6" dust port, twist-lock power inlet(s). | On the saw side, out of sight. |

Why this arrangement and not a straight 10-footer:

- The miter saw is the tallest, loudest, dustiest thing on the bench. Putting it
  on the wing keeps its hood off the outfeed edge entirely and gives the saw
  87" of board support on one side without any extension.
- The router feed direction runs along the island's front. With the router in
  the south-east quarter, a 6' board being routed never reaches the vises.
- Outfeed needs a clear path past the blade for a full 8' board. Zone B runs
  48" deep and then the alcove is open floor, so nothing ever gets in the way.
- The wing's east face can sit 24"–30" from a wall (that clearance is only for
  the hood and the wing's east-side doors); the island's south face is where
  you actually stand.

## 4. Dust collection strategy

Principles: one trunk, short branches, wyes not tees, long-sweep bends, a blast
gate at every drop, cabinets sealed so the suction goes where you want it.

- **Main trunk: 6" spiral or PVC**, straight, in the duct chase along the north
  strip. It exits the north face at the NE corner through a 6" flanged port.
  At 4,000 ft/min a 6" pipe moves about 785 CFM; that is the target for the
  collector at the bench inlet (a 2 HP+ cyclone with a 6" inlet, or a 1.5 HP
  unit with the 5" trunk option below).
- **Branches, west to east along the trunk** (each with a blast gate, each a 45°
  wye entering in the direction of flow toward the port):
  1. Floor sweep — 4" gate at the west toe kick. Sweep the alcove into it.
  2. Downdraft plenum — 4" gate. Only open while sanding.
  3. Table saw — 4" hose from the CNS's own port to a gate on the island's north
     face, right behind the blade. Later, an over-arm guard hose can share it
     with a 2-1/2" reducer.
  4. Router cabinet — 4" from the sealed router box, plus a 2-1/2" hose from the
     fence port teed in ahead of the gate. The box has a filtered make-up-air
     inlet so it doesn't starve.
  5. Miter hood — 4" drop from the hood, run down inside the wing's east side
     and north along the wing to the trunk. Plus 2-1/2" from the saw's chute.
- **Sizing for your collector.** If your collector is a 4"-port 1 HP machine,
  the trunk becomes 5" and the plan tells you to open one gate at a time. If
  it is a 6" cyclone, the trunk stays 6" and two gates can be open (router
  fence + cabinet, or miter hood + chute).
- **Clean-outs**: capped ends of the trunk at both ends of the chase, reachable
  from the west end door and the NE corner.
- **Optional automation**: iVac-style current-sensing tool switches at the
  router and miter outlets that open their gate and start the collector.

## 5. Electrical strategy

- Feed: one or two 20 A / 120 V circuits arrive at the NE corner through
  twist-lock (L5-20) inlets on the north face. One flexible SO cord to the
  wall or a floor box. Nothing else leaves the bench. (240 V for the collector
  stays at the wall unless you want it routed through — question 10.)
- Inside: metal raceway in the service chase; every box metal, everything
  grounded to the raceway.
- Circuit A: router receptacle inside the router cabinet, paddle switch on
  its door, duplex outlets on the west end and two flip-lid duplexes on the
  south apron.
- Circuit B: miter saw receptacle inside the well, paddle switch on the wing's
  alcove face, duplex on the wing's south end, charging drawer (switched, with
  a ventilation slot) and one duplex on the north face for the table saw.
- Cords: every built-in tool plugs in inside its own cabinet. Cord loops are
  strapped to the chase wall, not lying in the sawdust.

## 7. Wear, durability and keeping it clean

- Top: hard maple, grain running the long way in each leg, edges eased to a
  3/16" round so nothing chips. Finish is hardwax oil, re-oiled once a year
  by wiping. A glue-up mat protects the assembly zone.
- The outfeed edge gets a 1/8" bevel on the top corner and is the only edge
  that is dead flush with the cabinet below, so stock never catches.
- Dog holes are chamfered top and bottom; the plenum means chips fall through
  and get sucked out instead of packing the holes.
- Fronts are 3/4" Baltic birch with a hard finish or laminate, finger pulls
  are routed into the top edge (nothing to catch a hip or a clamp).
- Drawers on 100 lb full-extension undermount slides, soft-close.
- Toe kicks are recessed and clad in black HDPE so brooms and boots don't
  mark them.
- All cabinet interiors are sealed with two coats of finish (dust wipes out).
- Levelling feet on steel plates, not casters. Question 11 covers mobility.

## 9. Things you may be missing — my suggestions

Tell me yes/no on each:

1. **Downdraft sanding through the dog holes** (already in the concept).
2. **Sharpening pull-out** on the west end: a drawer-mounted station at bench
   height with a water tray, so sharpening stops being a chore you avoid.
3. **Floor-sweep gate** at the toe kick.
4. **Charging drawer** for battery packs with a switched outlet and vent.
5. **Tilt-out offcut bin** so small cutoffs never pile up on the top.
6. **Stop-track rule on the miter fence line** (an aluminium T-track with a
   flip stop and an adhesive tape rule, along the wing's fence line), so
   repeat cuts don't need a pencil.
7. **Long-item pull-out** at the wing's north end for straightedges, levels and
   track-saw rails.
8. **Air line** quick-connect at the utility corner if you own a compressor.
9. **Assembly mat and cauls** stored flat in the wing.
10. **A single "kill" switch** at the alcove that de-energises both circuits.
11. **LED task strip** under a short shelf at the miter station only (the one
    place a shadow line helps).
12. **Vise choice**: I've drawn a quick-release face vise and a wagon vise.
    A leg vise or a twin-screw are alternatives; see question 6.

Things I deliberately left out: a tool trough (collects chips, breaks the
flat top), built-in drill press or planer (they conflict with a flat slab),
casters as the default (a 1,000 lb bench should sit still).

## 10. Questions for you

Please answer as many as you can; short answers are fine.

1. **Space and placement.** Roughly how big is the shop, and where would this
   go? Which side can be near a wall? Where does the dust collector live
   relative to it, and where is the nearest circuit or panel? Slab floor or
   wood?
2. **Miter saw.** Make and model? Are you happy with it living permanently in
   the well (arm sticking up above the top at the end of the wing), or do you
   want a lift-out plate so the well can be covered for glue-ups?
3. **Router and lift.** Which router do you have, and do you own a lift or
   should I specify one (JessEm Mast-R-Lift II, Woodpeckers, Incra, or a
   Triton-style router with built-in lift)?
4. **Dust collector.** Make, model, port size, and does it have a 6" inlet?
   Do you also run a shop vac for the 2-1/2" ports? Interested in automatic
   gates?
5. **Dog holes.** 3/4" (traditional holdfasts, Veritas/Benchcrafted dogs) or
   20 mm on a 96 mm grid (Festool/MFT and track-saw accessories)? This
   changes the whole grid and the vise choice.
6. **Vises.** Quick-release face vise + wagon vise as drawn, or a leg vise /
   twin-screw / Moxon? Are you right- or left-handed?
7. **Clamps (item 11).** I read "Clamp" as clamp storage. Confirm? And roughly
   what you own: how many parallel clamps and their lengths, F-clamps, pipe
   clamps, spring clamps. Should all of them live in the bench or only the
   ones you reach for constantly?
8. **Top.** Full hard maple on both legs, or the hybrid (maple only on the
   workbench zone)? Is maple easy to buy where you are, or would ash / beech /
   white oak be cheaper?
9. **Look.** Two-tone (light maple top, charcoal fronts) as rendered, or a
   different pairing (walnut fronts, natural birch with exposed plywood
   edges)? Finger pulls or exposed handles?
10. **Electrical.** Can you bring two 20 A circuits to it? Any 240 V needs at
    the bench?
11. **Mobility.** Stationary on levelling feet (my recommendation) or do you
    need retractable casters to reconfigure the shop occasionally?
12. **The extras** in section 9: which do you want?
13. **Your table saw as installed.** Measure the table height from the floor
    and the height of the top of the rear fence rail. That decides the outfeed
    lip detail.
